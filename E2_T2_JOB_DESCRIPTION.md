# E2 · T2 — Job Description

Replaces `## Task T2 — Job description` in the E2 engine module.

```
## Task T2 — Job description

Describe the job. Do not describe the ideal human.

The first attracts people who can do the work. The second attracts people who \
are good at recognising themselves in adjectives, and quietly deters everyone \
who reads a long requirements list and counts what they are missing.

### What the level means

The level is given. Hold it consistently — these are the expectations each one \
carries, and every part of the description must match:

- Intern: learning in a structured way, supervised, contributing to defined \
pieces of work.
- Junior: executes defined work with guidance, owns small tasks end to end, \
escalates early.
- Mid: owns a workstream independently, makes routine judgement calls alone, \
escalates the genuinely ambiguous.
- Senior: owns ambiguous problems, sets the approach rather than receiving it, \
raises the standard of work around them.
- Lead: accountable for outcomes across several people or workstreams, owns \
delivery in their area, develops the people in it.
- Head of: owns a function, sets its direction and budget, builds and hires \
the team.

Check the requirements against the level before you deliver. A Junior role \
asking for six years of experience, or a Mid role asking for team leadership, \
is not a demanding advertisement — it is a mismatch that either deters the \
right applicants or misprices the job. Where the supplied context implies one, \
write the description to the level given and flag the contradiction in one \
line at the end, addressed to the hiring manager rather than the candidate.

### Build it in this order

1. WHAT THE ROLE IS FOR — two or three sentences on the problem this person \
is being hired to solve. Not the company's history. The reason the seat exists.
2. THE FIRST NINETY DAYS — what they will actually do, concretely. This is the \
section candidates read most carefully, because it is the only one that tells \
them what the job feels like.
3. WHAT THEY OWN AFTER A YEAR — the scope once they are established, at the \
level stated.
4. HOW SUCCESS IS MEASURED — in terms someone could put a number or a date \
against. "Doing a good job" is not a measure. If the context gives no \
measures, say what would ordinarily be measured for this kind of work and \
mark it for the hiring manager to confirm.
5. WHO THEY WORK WITH — the team, and who they report to.
6. REQUIRED — see the test below.
7. USEFUL — everything that failed the test.
8. WHAT THE COMPANY OFFERS — pay, location, working pattern, and anything the \
context supplies. A description that asks for everything and offers nothing \
is answered in kind.
9. HOW TO APPLY — what to send and what happens next.

### Requirements — the test

Apply this to every line you are about to put under REQUIRED: would you turn \
down an otherwise outstanding candidate solely for missing this?

If no, it is USEFUL, and it goes under that heading. A list of twelve \
requirements is nearly always two requirements and ten preferences, and the \
difference decides who applies. Keep REQUIRED to five entries at most.

Never write a requirement as a number of years where the underlying need is a \
capability. "Has run a payroll for a company of this size" tells a candidate \
what you mean; "5+ years' experience" tells them to count.

### What must never be invented

You know nothing about this company beyond the context supplied. Never state \
its mission, values, size, funding, customers, products or history from your \
own knowledge.

Pay range, location, working pattern, reporting line and the application \
process are the five things candidates most need and the five most often \
missing. Where the context does not supply one, write \
[NEEDS INPUT: pay range] and leave it visible.

Never write "competitive salary". It is the absence of the figure dressed as \
a benefit, and it is precisely the plausible default this rule exists to \
prevent. The same applies to "market rate" and "salary depending on \
experience".

### Language

Delete on sight: rockstar, ninja, guru, wizard, hit the ground running, wear \
many hats, fast-paced environment, work hard play hard, we are like a family, \
thrives under pressure, must be able to juggle. Each says nothing, or says \
something the writer would not say out loud.

Separately, and for a different reason: where a phrase reads as a personality \
test rather than a description of work — aggressive, dominant, relentless, \
crushing it, hungry — replace it with the behaviour it is pointing at. These \
measurably narrow who applies, and they rarely describe anything the job \
needs.

Write to the candidate, in the second person, in plain sentences. Keep the \
whole description under 700 words; past that it stops being read and starts \
being skimmed for reasons to stop.

### Before you return

Check silently. Does every requirement pass the test. Is anything claimed \
about the company that the context did not supply. Are pay, location, working \
pattern and reporting line either stated or bracketed. Is the scope described \
consistent with the level given. Does the description say what the person will \
do, rather than what they should already be.
```
