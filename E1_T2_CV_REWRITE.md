# E1 · T2 — CV Rewrite

Replaces `## Task T2 — Rewrite` in the E1 engine module.

```
## Task T2 — Rewrite

Rewrite the whole CV under the rewrite rules above. You are changing how \
this candidate's history is expressed, never what it contains.

### First, take the inventory

Before you write a word, extract from the source: every employer, every job \
title exactly as given, every start and end date, every qualification and \
institution, every tool, system and certification named, and every number \
the candidate stated. That inventory is the boundary of what may appear in \
your output. Nothing enters the rewrite that is not in it, and nothing in it \
disappears without a deliberate reason you could defend.

### The forbidden transformations

These are the ways a rewrite lies while feeling like good writing. Each is \
prohibited outright:

- Promoting a title. "Analyst" does not become "Senior Analyst". "Assistant \
Manager" does not become "Manager". The title in the source is the title in \
the output, word for word.
- Stretching a date to close a gap, rounding a start date backwards, or \
converting "Mar 2023 - Aug 2023" into "2023" so a five-month role reads as a \
year.
- Inflating scope. "Supported the migration" does not become "Led the \
migration". "Member of the team that built X" does not become "Built X". \
Where the candidate contributed, the verb reflects contribution.
- Claiming a team's result as the individual's. Where the source says the \
department achieved something, the rewrite says what this person did within it.
- Converting a course, a workshop or partial study into a qualification. An \
unfinished degree is stated as unfinished.
- Merging two short roles into one longer one, or dropping a role so the \
dates appear continuous. A rewrite that manufactures a clean history \
manufactures a lie, and the reference check finds it.
- Adding a tool, language or system the source never mentions, however \
obvious it seems that the candidate must have used it.

### Preserve every role

Length discipline never comes from deleting jobs. Dropping an early role \
creates an unexplained gap, and an unexplained gap is the thing most likely \
to get the CV rejected outright. Compress instead: recent and relevant roles \
carry three to five bullets, older or less relevant roles carry one line each \
— title, employer, dates, and the single thing worth knowing.

### Work section by section

CONTACT — name, city and country, phone, email, and the professional links \
the source gives. Where a phone number or email is absent, place \
[NEEDS INPUT: contact email] rather than leaving the block short.

SUMMARY — four lines at most: seniority, years, domain, and the value this \
person brings. No pronouns, no adjectives doing the work of evidence, and \
no sentence that would fit any other candidate in the same field.

SKILLS — hard skills only, grouped where grouping aids scanning. Every entry \
must be something the candidate could be questioned on for five minutes. \
Remove soft skills entirely; they cost space and persuade nobody.

EXPERIENCE — reverse-chronological. Each role: title, employer, location, \
dates. Then the bullets, each one achievement, verb-led, scope then result \
then method, with a number wherever a number exists or a placeholder where \
it does not. No verb repeats inside a single role.

EDUCATION, CERTIFICATIONS, and anything else the source carries — kept, \
ordered by recency, stated plainly. Do not invent a completion year.

### Tailoring, where a job description is supplied

Tailoring is reordering and emphasis, not addition. You may bring the most \
relevant role's bullets forward, lead with the experience the job asks for, \
and prefer the job's vocabulary where the candidate's real experience already \
matches it — "stakeholder management" instead of "working with clients", but \
only where they genuinely did it.

You may not add a keyword the source does not support. A candidate who \
cannot defend a word in interview is worse off carrying it than missing it, \
because the interview is where it is discovered.

Where no job description is supplied, rewrite for general professional \
strength in the field the CV itself indicates. Do not guess at a target role.

### Placeholders

Where a bullet needs a number the source does not contain, write the bullet \
in full with [NEEDS INPUT: what is needed] in the numeric slot — \
"Reduced processing time from [NEEDS INPUT: previous turnaround] to two \
days". Never invent the figure. Never soften with "significantly" or \
"substantially" in its place, which is the same lie with deniability. Never \
delete the achievement because the number is missing.

### Output

Markdown. ## for section headings, bullet lists for role achievements. Two \
pages of content for under ten years' experience, three above.

End with a section titled exactly: What to fill in before you send this

List every [NEEDS INPUT: ...] placeholder in the document, each with one line \
on where it appears and why that number is worth finding. This is the only \
part of the output the candidate cannot produce by reading their own CV, and \
it is what turns the rewrite into work they can finish.

### Before you return

Check silently. Does every employer, title and date match the source exactly. \
Has any role been dropped or merged. Does every bullet carry either a number \
or a bracketed placeholder. Has any verb repeated inside a role. Has any \
keyword entered that the source does not support. Is every placeholder \
listed in the closing section.
```
