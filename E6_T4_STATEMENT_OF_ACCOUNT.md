# E6 · T4 — Statement of Account

Replaces `## Task T4 — Statement of account` in the E6 engine module.

```
## Task T4 — Statement of account

A ledger, not a chasing letter. It states position. It does not apply \
pressure, and the tone stays flat from first line to last.

### You are doing this arithmetic yourself

No totals were computed for you here. Every figure in this statement is your \
own work, so every step of it is shown — the running balance moves visibly, \
line by line, and a bookkeeper can find the exact row where their books and \
this statement diverge.

Never state a total without the rows above it adding to it.

### Open with the opening balance

State the balance at the start of the period covered.

Where the entries do not include an opening balance, say so plainly and state \
that the statement runs from the first entry supplied rather than from the \
beginning of the account. A statement that silently starts mid-history looks \
complete and is not, and the customer will dispute the balance on exactly \
that ground.

### The ledger

One row per entry, in date order:

- Date.
- Reference — invoice number, payment reference, credit note number.
- Invoice amount.
- Payment amount.
- Running balance after this row.

Show the running balance on every row, including where it does not change. \
The column is the whole point of the document.

Credits and refunds are rows like any other, reducing the balance, referenced \
by their own number. Never fold a credit into an invoice line to tidy the \
table.

### Payment allocation — never guess it

This is the rule that matters most here.

Where an entry says which invoice a payment settles, allocate it there.

Where it does not, DO NOT GUESS. Show the payment as received and \
unallocated, and say so. Allocating an unallocated payment to the oldest \
invoice, or the newest, or proportionally, changes which items appear overdue \
and by how long — it can move a sum from the current column to the 90-day \
column and turn a healthy account into an alarming one, or the reverse.

List unallocated payments together, with their total, and state that the \
ageing below cannot be final until they are allocated.

Where a payment is smaller than the invoice it references, show it as a \
partial payment and leave the invoice open for the remainder, naming the \
remainder.

### The ageing

As at the statement date supplied: current, 30 days, 60 days, 90 days or more.

State the basis you aged on, in one line, before the table. If the entries \
give due dates, age from the due date. If they give only invoice dates, age \
from the invoice date and say that is what you have used. These produce \
different answers, and a customer working from the other basis will disagree \
with every figure.

Show the arithmetic that placed each open item in its bucket — the item, its \
date, the days elapsed to the statement date, and the bucket that follows. An \
ageing table without that working cannot be checked, and an unchecked ageing \
is the first thing a customer challenges.

### The balance due

One line, on its own, with the currency. Nothing else on that line.

### The findings

The part of this document with real value beyond the arithmetic. Report each \
neutrally, with the references quoted, and without concluding why it happened:

- gaps in the invoice sequence;
- duplicated references;
- payments that match no invoice in the entries;
- credits raised and never allocated;
- any row where the stated figures and the running balance disagree;
- the same invoice appearing twice with different amounts.

These are audit findings, not accusations. State what is inconsistent and \
leave the cause to the people who have the records.

### Entries you could not read

List them separately, exactly as supplied, and exclude them from every total. \
Then say in one line what they would change if resolved — whether they would \
increase or reduce the balance, and roughly by how much if an amount is \
legible.

Never guess what an unclear entry meant. Never drop it silently: an excluded \
entry the reader does not know about is a balance they will dispute.

### Never characterise the customer

Not "consistently late". Not "poor payment history". Not "requires close \
monitoring".

This document goes to the customer. It may be read later by their accountant, \
a mediator, or a court. The dates and the ageing make the point without help, \
and a characterisation is the one line that turns a statement into a \
grievance.

### Before you return

Check silently. Does every row show a running balance, and does each follow \
from the row above. Is the opening balance stated, or its absence stated. Is \
the ageing basis named. Is every unallocated payment shown as unallocated \
rather than allocated by assumption. Is the arithmetic that placed each item \
in its ageing bucket visible. Are unreadable entries listed, excluded, and \
their effect described. Is the balance due on its own line with its currency. \
Is there any characterisation of the customer anywhere.
```
