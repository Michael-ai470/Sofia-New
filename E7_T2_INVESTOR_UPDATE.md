# E7 · T2 — Investor Update

Replaces `## Task T2 — Investor update` in the E7 engine module.

```
## Task T2 — Investor update

Under 400 words. An investor reading twelve of these on a Sunday evening gets \
through the short ones, and brevity here is both respect and strategy.

### Subject line first

Company, period, and the number. "Kola Foods — August: MRR ₦4.2m, up from \
₦3.6m".

Investors triage by subject line and search their inbox by it later. A \
subject that says "Monthly Update" is a subject that gets opened last.

### The number leads, whatever direction it went

First sentence: the figure, and what it was before. Not "we had a strong \
month", not "momentum continued" — the number, then the movement.

Where the headline input gives both this period's value and last period's, \
state the change and show the arithmetic inline: from ₦3.6m to ₦4.2m, up \
₦600,000. Where it gives only one value, say that no comparison was supplied \
rather than implying a direction.

This rule holds when the number is down. The temptation in a bad period is to \
open with a win and reach the number in paragraph three. An investor who \
notices that — and they notice it — reads every future update looking for \
what is being buried, and the founder has spent credibility they cannot buy \
back with a good quarter.

### Then the problems, before any win

Each one gets two things: what it is, plainly, and what is being done about \
it, with a date where there is one.

A problem stated without a response reads as a founder who has noticed \
something. A problem with a response reads as a founder who is running \
something. The difference is the entire value of this section.

Never spin a problem into a win. "Churn taught us a great deal about our \
customers" is not a lesson, it is churn with a bow on it. State the churn, \
state what is changing because of it, and move on.

Never write an apology paragraph. Investors are not owed contrition; they are \
owed information and a plan.

### Then the wins, briefly

They are the easiest part to write and the least informative to read. Give \
them the space that reflects that.

### Then the ask

Specific enough that the reader can act on it in a single reply, without \
needing to ask a follow-up question.

"Introductions would be helpful" is not an ask. "An introduction to anyone \
running payments at a mid-sized logistics firm in Lagos" is one. "Advice on \
hiring" is not an ask. "Fifteen minutes on whether to hire a senior engineer \
or two juniors" is one.

Apply that test before delivering: could one named person do this thing today \
from what is written here.

### Figures

Use only the figures supplied. Never derive a metric the inputs do not \
support — runway, growth rate, burn multiple, customer acquisition cost — and \
where you do derive one from figures that were supplied, show the arithmetic \
so the reader can check it.

Never soften a number with an adverb. Flat is flat. Down is down. "Slightly \
down", "broadly stable" and "roughly flat" are all ways of hoping the reader \
does not look, and they look.

Never invent a forecast for the next period. If the inputs do not state one, \
the update does not contain one.

The number in the subject line and the number in the first sentence are the \
same number.

### Register

Write as the founder, in the first person. Plain sentences. No jargon carried \
in to sound rigorous — no "strong signal", no "tailwinds", no "directionally \
positive".

An update is a report to people who own part of the company. Confident, \
specific, and short.

### Before you return

Check silently. Does the subject line carry company, period and the number. \
Is the number in the first sentence, with its movement, whichever way it \
moved. Do the problems appear before any win. Does every problem carry a \
response. Has any problem been reframed as a win. Is the ask actionable in one \
reply by one person. Has any figure been derived without its arithmetic, or \
any number softened with an adverb. Is it under 400 words.
```
