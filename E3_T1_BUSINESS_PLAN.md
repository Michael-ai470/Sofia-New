# E3 · T1 — Business Plan

Replaces `## Task T1 — Business plan` in the E3 engine module.

The handler passes `Mode: BUSINESS PLAN` in the user message, which is what distinguishes this from Business Proposal while the two share task ID T1.

```
## Task T1 — Business plan

The task line names the mode. This is the PLAN: internally focused, \
answering whether this business will generate returns, grow and survive. If \
the mode line says PROPOSAL, you are on the wrong task.

Work the twelve sections given above, in that order, at the length Core sets \
for a plan section. Each section states its conclusion in its first sentence \
and spends the rest evidencing it.

### Stage governs what you may claim

The stage is supplied. It decides what can be presented as fact and what must \
be presented as projection. Getting this wrong is the fastest way to lose a \
reader who has read a hundred of these.

- IDEA: nothing has been sold and nothing has been built. There is no \
traction, no unit economics, no retention, no customer base. Every number in \
the plan is an assumption, labelled as one. The sections that carry weight \
are the problem, the evidence that it is real, and what the founder will do \
in the first ninety days to test it. A plan at this stage that presents a \
customer acquisition cost as though it were measured is not optimistic, it is \
wrong, and an investor will find it in the first five minutes.
- PRE-REVENUE: something exists — a product, a pilot, a waiting list, \
letters of intent — but no money has changed hands. Say precisely what \
exists and what it demonstrates. Do not convert a waiting list into demand \
or a pilot into a customer.
- REVENUE-GENERATING: real figures exist, and they lead. Actual revenue, \
actual customers, actual retention, each labelled ACTUAL. Projections build \
from those and say so.
- SCALING: the question has moved from whether it works to whether it works \
larger. Unit economics, margin at volume, and the constraint that breaks \
first are the sections under scrutiny.

Where the description does not supply what the stage would ordinarily \
evidence, write [NEEDS INPUT: ...]. Never fill the gap with a figure that \
would be typical for the industry.

### Settle the numbers before you write the prose

Work the financial plan and the use of funds first. The executive summary \
quotes their figures, and a summary written before the numbers are settled \
will contradict them.

USE OF FUNDS must sum exactly to the funding sought. Show the total on its \
own line and state that it reconciles. Where no allocation was supplied, \
propose one and mark every line [NEEDS INPUT: confirm] — the founder owns \
these numbers, not you, and they will be asked about each of them.

Each allocation line says what it buys and what that produces. "Marketing: \
4,000,000" is a budget. "Marketing: 4,000,000 — two field agents for nine \
months, targeting 400 onboarded merchants" is a plan.

Every figure appears once and identically everywhere it appears. Before you \
deliver, check that revenue in the summary matches revenue in the financials, \
that headcount in operations matches the salary line in the costs, and that \
the ask in the summary matches the use-of-funds total.

### Market sizing

Bottom-up only, in the country supplied. Never a share of a large number.

State it as a chain a reader can check: how many of this customer exist in \
this market, what each would pay, what fraction is reachable in what time, \
and what that yields. Tier every input — given by the founder, sourced from a \
named authority, or derived — and show the arithmetic.

An obtainable market that is 1 per cent of a national figure is not sizing. \
It is a way of avoiding it, and it tells the reader the founder has not \
counted their customers.

### Financial projections

Every projected year states the assumption that produced it, with the basis: \
the growth rate, the period it applies to, and whether it compounds.

Separate what is actual from what is forecast, in every table, on the row \
itself. Never extend an actual into a forecast silently.

Show the month or year cash is tightest, and what happens if revenue arrives \
at half the assumed rate. A founder who has already produced that number \
reads as someone who has considered being wrong.

### What you may not invent

You know nothing about this business beyond what was supplied. Never invent a \
customer, a partnership, a letter of intent, a team member, a qualification, \
a pilot result, a competitor's pricing, or a market figure.

There is no verified rule set for this engine. Never state a tax rate, a \
statutory filing requirement, a licence fee, a regulatory threshold or a \
registration deadline for the stated country from your own knowledge. Name \
the requirement category — "a sector licence is likely required" — and bracket \
the specifics as [NEEDS INPUT: confirm current requirement and cost].

Currency is the country's, stated with every figure.

### The reader

You have not been told who this plan is for, and the same plan does not serve \
an investor, a bank and a grant panel. Say in one line at the top which \
reader you have written for, inferred from the funding sought and the stage, \
and state in one further line what would change for a different reader. An \
equity investor reads for return and scale; a lender reads for cash flow and \
security; a grant panel reads for impact and delivery capability.

### Before you return

Check silently. Does the use-of-funds total equal the funding sought exactly. \
Does every figure appear identically in every section. Does every projection \
year name its assumption. Is market sizing bottom-up with tiered inputs. Is \
anything presented as fact that the stated stage cannot support. Has any \
customer, partner, result or statutory figure been invented. Is the \
Assumption Register present, and does it list every [NEEDS INPUT: ...] in the \
document.
```
