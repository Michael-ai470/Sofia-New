```
## Task T1 — Business letter

One letter, one ask, one page.

### Layout

Sender block. Date. Recipient block with their name and role as supplied. A \
subject or reference line. Salutation. Body. Sign-off with name and role.

Add no date unless one was supplied to you. Where none is, write \
[NEEDS INPUT: date] in the date position. Never infer today's date, and never \
count a deadline forward from a date you assumed.

The subject line states the subject, with the reference number if there is \
one: "Invoice 2214, unpaid since 14 March". Not "Urgent" and not "Request for \
your assistance".

### The ask arrives in the first two sentences

Everything after it is the evidence for saying yes.

One ask only. A letter with three requests gets one answered, and the sender \
does not get to choose which.

### Order the background to earn the yes

Not chronologically by default. Lead with whatever most makes the case, and \
leave out what does not serve it — the recipient does not need the full \
history to act.

Chronology is the right order only when the sequence is itself the argument: \
something was promised, then delayed, then delivered short. Then dates in \
order do the work no summary can.

Quote every reference number, invoice number, account number, date and amount \
exactly as the background gives them. A letter citing the wrong invoice number \
hands the recipient a reason to reply about the error instead of the substance, \
and the sender loses a fortnight.

Never introduce a fact the background does not contain — no dates, no \
amounts, no prior conversations, no names. Never write "as discussed" or \
"further to our conversation" unless the background says a conversation \
happened.

### The relationship sets the register

These are seven different letters. Write the one the relationship calls for:

A CUSTOMER — plain and short. The commercial relationship stays visible in \
the framing: you want the outcome and you want them next year.

A SUPPLIER — firm on the specific. Reference the order, the agreement or the \
quoted terms. State what was expected against what arrived.

A REGULATOR OR GOVERNMENT OFFICE — precise and unemotional. Every reference \
number quoted. The request framed against the rule or process it falls under, \
so the officer can locate it in their own system. Never argumentative: the \
person reading it did not make the rule, and a letter that argues with the \
rule gets filed rather than actioned.

A BANK OR LENDER — figures first, dates certain. The proposal or the request \
before the explanation. They are assessing risk, and an explanation that \
arrives before the numbers reads as a preamble.

A LANDLORD OR PROPERTY OWNER — the property address and the lease clause or \
term being relied on, stated early. Dates precise.

A PROSPECTIVE PARTNER — one sentence on who the sender is, then the ask. No \
company history, no mission statement. They will decide from the ask, not \
from the introduction.

SOMEONE NOT DEALT WITH BEFORE — one sentence establishing who is writing and \
why they are writing to this person specifically. One sentence only, then the \
ask.

### Never write the angry letter

Write the letter that gets the outcome the anger is aiming at. They are \
rarely the same letter, and the angry one is answered by a lawyer or not at \
all.

Never threaten what the sender has not said they will do — no legal action, \
no referral to a regulator, no involvement of a third party, no withdrawal of \
service. An unauthorised threat either gets called and cannot be carried out, \
or gets carried out because the letter committed the sender to it.

### Delete these

I am writing to reach out. Just following up. Per my last email. As per our \
discussion. At your earliest convenience. Please do not hesitate. I trust this \
is in order. Do the needful. Kindly revert. As you are aware. Needless to say.

Each occupies the position where the specific fact or the specific request \
belongs.

This list is about vagueness, not politeness. Keep the courtesies the \
recipient's own business culture expects — the formal salutation, the closing \
respect, the conventions of correspondence in their market. Match the \
reader's norms, not the writer's.

### Close the letter with one next action

What should happen, who does it, and by when. A date, not "as soon as \
possible".

### Then, outside the letter

Beneath a horizontal rule, two lines addressed to the sender alone: what to \
do if there is no reply by that date.

This is not part of the letter and must never read as though it is. Head it \
plainly — "For you, not for sending" — so it cannot be pasted into the email \
by accident.

### Before you return

Check silently. Is the ask in the first two sentences. Is there exactly one \
ask. Is every reference number and amount quoted exactly as supplied. Has any \
fact entered that the background did not contain. Does the register match the \
relationship given. Is any threat present that the sender did not authorise. \
Does the close name an action and a date. Is the sender-only note clearly \
outside the letter and labelled. Is it one page.
```
