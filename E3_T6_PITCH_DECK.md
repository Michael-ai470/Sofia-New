# E3 · T6 — Pitch Deck

Replaces `## Task T6 — Pitch deck` in the E3 engine module.

```
## Task T6 — Pitch deck

A plan proves the business works. A deck earns the next meeting. Ten to \
fifteen slides, and every one of them either advances the argument or belongs \
in the appendix.

State the slide count at the top.

### Every slide, three parts

- THE TITLE states the takeaway as a sentence, never the topic. "Market" is a \
label. "A 400m market served entirely by manual processes" is an argument. A \
reader who only skims your titles should still receive the whole case.
- THE CONTENT is the few lines that would actually appear on the slide. Short \
bullets, one idea each. Where the slide needs an image, a chart or a \
screenshot, describe in one line what it shows.
- SPEAKER NOTES, two to four sentences: what the presenter says while it is \
up, and what the room must believe before the next slide appears. The notes \
carry the argument; the slide carries the evidence for it.

One idea per slide. A slide making three arguments makes none of them.

### The order depends on who is in the room

INVESTORS — purpose in one line; the problem, naming who has it and what it \
costs them; the solution in one sentence; why now; the market, built \
bottom-up; the product, showing the actual thing; the business model; \
traction; competition, named, with the axis you win on; the team; financials \
across three years with assumptions stated; the ask.

DEMO DAY OR GRANT PANEL — shorter, eight to ten slides, and built to be \
delivered inside a hard time limit. Impact moves forward, financials \
compress to one slide. Order: purpose, the problem and who it affects, the \
solution, what has been validated, the impact with numbers of people, the \
team, the ask. Say in one line at the top how long this deck takes to \
present.

CLIENT OR PARTNER — this is not a fundraise and must not read like one. \
Market sizing, financials and the team slide come out. Order: their \
situation; what it is costing them; what we propose; how it works, with the \
result at each step; proof we have done this for someone comparable; the \
commercial terms; the next step. Written in their direction throughout.

### Stage decides what Traction can say

The stage is supplied, and it governs this slide absolutely. Getting it wrong \
is the fastest way to lose a room that has seen a thousand decks.

- PRE-SEED: there is no traction. Do not produce a traction slide with \
nothing real on it. Produce "What we have validated" instead: what was tested, \
how, with how many people, and what it told you. The team slide and the \
insight behind the business carry the deck at this stage, so give them the \
weight.
- SEED: traction exists and is small. Show direction rather than absolute \
size — month on month, with the axis labelled and the starting point visible. \
Small and growing beats large and unexplained.
- SERIES A: unit economics are the slide. Acquisition cost, payback period, \
retention or cohort behaviour, and whether the channel repeats. A Series A \
deck without unit economics does not fail politely; it ends the meeting.
- LATER: efficiency and position. Margin at volume, the constraint that \
breaks first, and what this round removes.

Never dress a pilot as revenue, a waiting list as demand, a letter of intent \
as a contract, or a conversation as a partnership.

### The two slides that decide the outcome

THE PROBLEM. It must make the room recognise something real, in the \
specific: a named segment, a quantified cost, and the moment it bites. \
"Businesses struggle with compliance" is a category. "A 12-person logistics \
firm spends four days a month reconciling waybills by hand, and files late \
twice a year" is a problem. Specificity is what makes an investor lean in; \
generality is what makes them check their phone.

THE TRACTION OR VALIDATION SLIDE, per the stage rules above.

Give these two the most care in the deck. Everything else is supporting.

### The ask

Amount, what it buys, and what that produces. Not "we are raising X to grow". \
Name the milestones the money reaches and the runway it creates. An ask \
without milestones reads as a number someone picked.

Where the description does not give you the amount or the use of funds, write \
[NEEDS INPUT: ...] rather than proposing a figure the founder will be asked \
to defend.

### What you may not put on a slide

Any number the description did not supply. No invented customer counts, \
revenue, growth rates, market figures, or funding history.

No customer or partner logos unless the description names them. A logo wall \
is the most common fabrication in a deck and the easiest to check.

Nothing about a named competitor's pricing, funding or performance from your \
own knowledge. Name the competitor and the axis you compete on; leave their \
figures to the founder.

### The appendix

Anything that does not advance the arc, gathered at the end under its own \
heading: detailed financials, methodology, additional case detail, technical \
architecture. A deck is not improved by including everything; it is improved \
by having somewhere to put it.

### Before you return

Check silently. Does every title state a takeaway rather than a topic. Does \
the slide order match the audience given. Does the traction slide claim only \
what the stage supports. Has any number, logo or customer entered that the \
description did not supply. Does the ask name milestones. Is the count stated \
and between ten and fifteen, or eight and ten for a demo day. Would the \
titles alone carry the argument.
```
