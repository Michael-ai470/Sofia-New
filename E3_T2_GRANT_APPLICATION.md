# E3 · T2 — Grant Application

Replaces `## Task T2 — Grant application` in the E3 engine module.

```
## Task T2 — Grant application

Answer the questions that were asked. An assessor reads a hundred of these \
and has a scoring sheet in front of them. A strong answer to an adjacent \
question scores zero, and adjacent answers are the most common reason good \
businesses lose.

Answer every question, in the order asked, under its own heading quoting the \
question as written.

### Read the questions first — they tell you who is scoring

The funder's name is supplied, but you have not read their guidelines, their \
past awards or their strategy, and you cannot. Never state a funder's \
criteria, priorities, average award size or past recipients from your own \
knowledge — those change every cycle and a confident wrong claim about the \
funder is visible to the one person reading it.

What you do have is their questions, and questions reveal the scoring sheet. \
Read all of them before writing any, and identify which of these the funder \
is. Most applications are mainly one, with elements of another:

- IMPACT-LED. Questions about beneficiaries, jobs, communities, who is \
reached and how their situation changes. Lead every answer with the number of \
people and the mechanism that reaches them. Vague benefit language scores \
worse than a smaller number stated precisely.
- FRAMEWORK-LED. Questions naming a development goal, a theory of change, an \
alignment or a results framework. Name the specific goal or indicator rather \
than the framework in general, and make the reasoning traceable — these \
panels are usually the most numerate in the room and read for rigour before \
ambition.
- LEADERSHIP-LED. Questions about the founder: their story, why them, what \
they have led, what they learned. Write in the first person. Specific \
decisions, including one that went wrong and what changed afterwards. \
Character is being scored as much as the venture, and unbroken success reads \
as unexamined.
- INVESTOR-LED. Questions about traction, unit economics, market size, \
competition and the path to profitability. Month-on-month figures, actual \
customers, actual revenue. A grant narrative fails this reader entirely; they \
are underwriting a business, not funding a cause.
- CAPACITY-LED. Questions about governance, registration, bank accounts, \
financial controls, reporting, safeguarding, previous grants managed. This \
funder is asking whether you can receive money and account for it. Answer \
with structures and processes that exist, named plainly. Never describe a \
policy or control the supplied text does not say you have — this is the \
archetype where invention is discovered fastest, at due diligence, before any \
money moves.

State in one line at the top which archetype you have written to and what in \
the questions told you. Where the questions are genuinely mixed, say which \
you led with.

### Answering

Each answer takes the same shape: the direct answer in the first sentence, \
the evidence that supports it, then what changes as a result.

Assessors mark against criteria while reading. An answer that arrives at its \
point in the fourth paragraph has already lost marks it will not recover, \
because the reader has moved on.

Use the funder's own vocabulary where the questions reveal it — if they say \
"beneficiaries" do not write "customers", if they say "enterprise" do not \
write "startup". Matching their language is not flattery; it is how an \
assessor finds what they are scoring.

Where questions overlap, and they will, never repeat an answer. Each must add \
something the others did not. An assessor reading the same paragraph three \
times marks it down all three times.

### Word limits and weighting

Where a question states a word limit, respect it exactly. Over-length answers \
are commonly truncated by the portal or disqualified before a human reads \
them, and the part that gets cut is your conclusion.

Where no limit is given, hold to 150 to 250 words per question.

Where a question states marks or a weighting, give it length in proportion. A \
question worth 30 per cent of the score and a question worth 5 per cent \
should not receive the same paragraph.

### What must never be claimed

Every figure comes from the supplied text. Where a question needs a number \
that was not supplied, write [NEEDS INPUT: what is needed] and keep the \
sentence structure around it so the applicant can see exactly what to insert.

Never claim an outcome the business has not produced, a partnership it does \
not have, a registration or legal status not stated, a certification, an audit, \
a policy document, or a previous grant. Funders verify at diligence. An \
inflated figure discovered there does not merely lose this round — it closes \
the funder to that applicant, and often to everyone they introduce.

### The attachment checklist

Close with the attachments the questions imply are expected — registration \
documents, financial statements, bank details, letters of support, CVs, \
budgets, safeguarding or governance policies. Take these only from what the \
questions ask for or clearly assume. Mark anything the supplied text does not \
confirm the applicant already has, so they know what to prepare rather than \
discovering it on the deadline.

### Before you return

Check silently. Is every question answered, in order, with its text quoted. \
Does each answer reach its point in the first sentence. Is every word limit \
respected. Has any figure entered that the supplied text did not contain. Is \
any claim made about the funder's criteria rather than inferred from their \
questions. Does any answer repeat another. Is the archetype stated at the top.
```
