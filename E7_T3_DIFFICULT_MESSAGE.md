# E7 · T3 — Difficult Message

Replaces `## Task T3 — Difficult message` in the E7 engine module.

```
## Task T3 — Difficult message

Three versions, each under its own heading naming the strategy, each opening \
with one line on what it pursues and what it trades away.

### They differ in strategy, not in tone

Apply this test to every pair before delivering: could you turn one version \
into the other by changing the adjectives? If yes, they are the same message \
twice and the task is not done.

What each one actually does differently:

PRESERVE THE RELATIONSHIP — gives something up to keep the door open. It \
offers a route that lets the other side act without losing face, and it puts \
no hard deadline on them. Trades: time, and whatever was authorised to \
concede.

HOLD THE POSITION — gives nothing up. It states the facts, cites what was \
agreed, restates the expectation, and accepts that the other side will not \
enjoy reading it. Trades: goodwill.

FORCE A DECISION — sets a firm date with a stated consequence, and accepts \
that the answer may be no or that the relationship may end. Trades: the \
relationship itself, potentially.

All three carry the same facts and the same ask. Only the route changes. That \
is what makes them comparable, and comparing them is the whole point.

### Concede only what was authorised

Where the authorised concessions are empty, no version offers anything — \
including the Preserve version.

Preserve without a concession is still a real strategy: it preserves through \
framing, through what it declines to escalate, through giving the other side \
an interpretation they can accept, and through leaving the next move to them. \
It does not preserve by inventing a discount, an extension or a payment plan \
the sender never agreed to.

Offering unauthorised ground is the most damaging thing this task can do, \
because the user sends it before noticing, and then has to withdraw an offer \
they are already on record as making.

### The consequence in Force a Decision must also be authorised

A deadline needs something on the other side of it. Use only what the sender \
has said they will do.

Never invent an escalation — no referral to lawyers, no debt collection, no \
regulator, no public review, no withdrawal of service — unless the situation \
or the authorised concessions say so. An invented threat either gets called, \
and cannot be carried out, or gets carried out because the message committed \
the sender to it.

Where nothing has been authorised, Force a Decision still works: the \
consequence is that the sender proceeds on their own assumption — reallocates \
the slot, closes the file, treats the matter as declined — and says so \
plainly.

### Every version

- The ask in the first two sentences. One ask only.
- Bad news in the first paragraph. Burying it reads as cowardice or \
manipulation and is usually both.
- Facts only from the situation supplied. Never invent a date, an amount, a \
prior conversation or a name.
- A next action and a date at the end.
- Never angry. Force a Decision is a deadline, not a temper. Write the message \
that gets the outcome the anger is aiming at.
- Standing alone. The user will pick one and send it, so no version may refer \
to the others or to the fact that alternatives exist.

### Write for the channel given

EMAIL — a subject line that states the subject, short paragraphs, the ask \
visible without scrolling.

FORMAL LETTER — full layout, reference numbers from the situation, dated. Add \
no date unless one was supplied; otherwise [NEEDS INPUT: date].

SHORT MESSAGE — under 80 words, no salutation block, one ask, one date. \
Everything a letter would carry as context is cut, because the recipient is \
reading it on a phone between two other things. This is the hardest of the \
three to write and the one most often sent.

### What is at stake depends on who is receiving it

A CLIENT WHO HAS NOT PAID — the money and the future work are both live. \
Preserve protects the account; Force accepts losing it to recover the cash.

A CLIENT YOU MUST SAY NO TO — the goal is declining without closing the door. \
Say no in the first paragraph; the reason is one sentence, not a defence.

A SUPPLIER WHO HAS LET YOU DOWN — separate the remedy for this failure from \
the decision about continuing. Conflating them weakens both.

AN EMPLOYEE OR CONTRACTOR — proceed carefully. Performance, conduct and \
termination carry employment consequences that vary by jurisdiction, and a \
message sent now becomes evidence later. Say in one line, above the three \
versions, that anything touching performance or ending an engagement should \
be checked against local employment requirements before sending.

A BUSINESS PARTNER — the relationship is itself the asset, and there is \
usually a legal or financial entanglement behind it. Force a Decision here \
has larger consequences than elsewhere; say so in its trade-off line.

A LANDLORD — reference the property and the lease term being relied on. \
Occupancy is the risk, so Force a Decision is rarely the strongest route.

SOMEONE ELSE — work from the situation supplied and name in one line what you \
have assumed the relationship to be.

### Do not choose for them

Do not recommend a version, do not order them by preference, and do not hint \
at which you would send. Only the user knows what the relationship is worth, \
what else is in progress, and whether they can afford to lose this one.

### Before you return

Check silently. Are the three genuinely different strategies rather than \
three tones. Do all three carry the same facts and the same ask. Does any \
version offer a concession that was not authorised, including Preserve. Does \
Force a Decision rely on a consequence the sender did not authorise. Is bad \
news in the first paragraph of each. Is each written for the channel given, \
and is the short message actually under 80 words. Does any version reference \
the others. Has a recommendation crept in anywhere.
```
