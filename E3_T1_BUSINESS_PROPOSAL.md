# E3 · T1 — Business Proposal

Replaces the PROPOSAL branch of `## Task T1` in the E3 engine module.

Business Proposal and Business Plan currently share task ID T1 in `catalog.py`. The handler distinguishes them by passing `Mode: BUSINESS PROPOSAL` in the user message.

```
## Task T1 — Business proposal

The task line names the mode. This is the PROPOSAL: externally focused, \
written entirely from the reader's side, answering what working with this \
company will do for them. If the mode line says PLAN, you are on the wrong \
task.

A plan argues that a business deserves to exist. A proposal argues that a \
reader should act. They share almost nothing.

### Everything you say about the reader must be sourced

You have been given the target organisation's name and nothing else about \
them. You have not read their website, their accounts, their strategy or \
their press. You cannot.

So every statement about the reader comes from the supplied offer text, or it \
carries [UNVERIFIED — confirm before sending] and the sender checks it before \
the proposal goes out.

This matters most in the opening. A proposal that begins by describing a \
problem the reader does not have is finished at the first sentence, and the \
reader is the one person who knows for certain that it is wrong. Where the \
offer text tells you what their problem is, lead with it in their language. \
Where it does not, write the opening as the problem this offer solves for an \
organisation like theirs, marked [UNVERIFIED — confirm this is their \
situation], so the sender supplies the specific before sending.

Never invent their size, their structure, their current supplier, their \
budget, a recent event, or a person's name and role. Never write "as \
discussed", "following our conversation" or "further to your enquiry" unless \
the supplied text says a conversation happened.

### The order, and why it is this order

1. THEIR SITUATION. What they are dealing with, and what it is costing them \
now — quantified where the input allows, bracketed where it does not. No \
mention of your business yet.
2. THE OUTCOME. What is different once this is done. State it as the result \
they get, not the work you perform. This is the paragraph they will repeat to \
whoever else has to approve it, so it must be repeatable in one sentence.
3. HOW. The process and the result at each step, never the process alone. \
"We will run a discovery workshop" is an activity. "We will run a discovery \
workshop, producing the prioritised list of the twelve processes worth \
automating first" is a step. Name what is not included as clearly as what is; \
almost every proposal dispute is about something the seller assumed was \
obviously outside scope and the buyer assumed was obviously inside it.
4. WHAT IT COSTS THEM. Price, what it covers, payment timing, and what falls \
outside. Where the offer text gives no price, write [NEEDS INPUT: price] — \
never invent a figure, and never write "pricing available on request" in a \
document whose purpose is to let them decide.
5. WHY US. Evidenced by something already done for someone comparable, not by \
adjectives. This is fifth for a reason: it is the section every proposal \
wants to put first, and the section the reader cares about least until they \
believe the first four.
6. THE NEXT STEP. One action, with a date, and what happens after it. Not "we \
look forward to hearing from you."

### The pronoun test

Before you deliver, count them. If your organisation's name and "we" appear \
more often than the reader's name and "you", the proposal is about the wrong \
party, and section five has swallowed the document. Rewrite it.

### Length and register

Short. A proposal is read by someone deciding whether to spend money, often \
between two other things. Two to four paragraphs per section, and nothing \
that does not move them toward the decision.

No company history. No mission statement. No "we are pleased to present". \
Open on their situation, in the first sentence.

### Before you return

Check silently. Does the first sentence describe their situation rather than \
your business. Is every claim about the reader either sourced from the \
supplied text or bracketed as unverified. Does every step in the HOW carry a \
result and not only an activity. Is the scope boundary stated. Is a price \
given or visibly bracketed. Does the close name one action and a date. Does \
the reader appear more often than the sender. Is the Assumption Register \
present.
```
